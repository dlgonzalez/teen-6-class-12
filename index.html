📄 Transcript support (teacher / second listening)

If you have kids, you know that your laptop or your tablet has become a playmate, a babysitter and a teacher all rolled into one. But how much screen time is too much?

Ten years ago, we only had to worry about one screen: the television. Now they're everywhere. Astonishing numbers from HealthyChildren.org show kids as young as eight years old are spending seven hours a day being entertained on a screen.

Meet two-year-old Bella. Take a look around her house: it is filled with electronics — televisions, laptops, a desktop computer, iPads and phones. Like many kids her age, she's already a screen genius.

Living right next door is the Ganesh family. They decided very early on to keep their kids screen-free for the most part. They limited screen time. In their house there is one television, and cell-phone use is a half-hour maximum on their days off.

Instead, they spend time playing real games like Battleship. Research from the American Academy of Pediatrics has shown that when you take electronics away, children's creativity begins to blossom, their moods become brighter and they're more interested in physical play.

Eye contact and verbal conversation improve. No screen time can also enhance empathy in children. Better sleep patterns may develop, and kids may even experience a reduction in headaches.

Once an electronic addiction has developed, however, it can be hard to break. Distraction works well with kids: they may miss technology less if they have another fun physical activity or something else they enjoy.

So put down the two-dimensional world and get a grasp on reality.

05

🌈 Conditional Power: Grammar Masterclass

Three ways to talk about possible futures, imaginary situations and different pasts.

1st conditional

🌱 Real possibility

IF + PRESENT,
WILL + BASE VERB

Use it for a real or possible future situation and its likely result.

If I turn off notifications, I will concentrate better.

Meaning: This is possible. It may really happen.

2nd conditional

💭 Imaginary situation

IF + PAST,
WOULD + BASE VERB

Use it for imaginary, unlikely or hypothetical situations in the present or future.

If I were the principal, I would create phone-free breaks.

Meaning: Imagine this situation. It is not real now.

3rd conditional

⏳ Different past

IF + PAST PERFECT,
WOULD HAVE + PAST PARTICIPLE

Use it to imagine a different result for something in the past that did not happen.

If I had gone to bed earlier, I would have felt better.

Meaning: The past cannot change. We imagine another result.

🔍 Compare the same topic in all three conditionals

ConditionalExampleWhat it means
FirstIf I use my phone tonight, I will sleep less.A real future possibility.
SecondIf I didn't have my phone, I would read more.An imaginary situation now.
ThirdIf I hadn't used my phone last night, I would have slept better.A different past, but it did not happen.

🧩 How to build the sentence

IF CLAUSE = the condition / situation.

MAIN CLAUSE = the result.

If you take breaks (condition), you will feel less tired (result).

You can change the order:

You will feel less tired if you take breaks.

Use a comma when the if-clause comes first. Usually, no comma is needed when it comes second.

🚫 Common mistakes

  • ❌ If I will study, I will pass.
    ✅ If I study, I will pass.
  • ❌ If I would be principal, I would…
    ✅ If I were principal, I would…
  • ❌ If I would have known, I would have…
    ✅ If I had known, I would have…
  • ❌ I would have go…
    ✅ I would have gone…

1st conditional details

Positive: If + present simple, will + verb.

Negative: If I don't rest, I won't feel ready.

Question: What will you do if the internet stops working?

Other result forms: can, may, might or an imperative.

If you finish your homework, you can play.

2nd conditional details

Positive: If + past simple, would + verb.

Negative: If I didn't have Wi-Fi, I wouldn't spend so much time online.

Question: What would you do if you lost your phone?

In careful English, were is common for all subjects in hypothetical statements.

If I were you, I would take a break.

3rd conditional details

Positive: If + had + past participle, would have + past participle.

Negative: If I hadn't stayed up, I wouldn't have felt tired.

Question: What would you have done if your phone had run out of battery?

It often expresses regret, criticism or an imagined alternative.

If we had left earlier, we would have arrived on time.

🧠 Quick meaning check

First: Is it possible? Yes, it may happen.
Second: Is it imaginary or unlikely? Yes, imagine it.
Third: Is it about a past that cannot change? Yes, imagine another past.

Remember: the past form in the second conditional does not always mean past time. It shows distance from reality. In the third conditional, the past perfect and “would have” refer to an unreal past.

🗣️ Talk to an Expert

Choose a specialist, discuss technology and bring back useful ideas for your classmates.

Group mission · 20–30 min

🎯 Your mission

  1. Form a group and choose one expert.
  2. Open the expert room using the link on the expert's card.
  3. Introduce your group and explain what you want to learn.
  4. Ask questions, listen carefully and give your own opinions. Do not let one person do all the talking.
  5. Ask the expert for a final report or summary of your conversation.
  6. Prepare a short group presentation to share the most useful ideas with the class.
Speaking mission: Try to use at least one first conditional, one second conditional and one third conditional during your conversation. Use them naturally; do not force them into every answer.

📝 Group report: capture what you learned

Use the expert's final report to complete this organizer. Then present the information in your own words.











Presentation tip: Do not read the report word for word. Explain the ideas naturally and make sure every group member contributes.

❓ Helpful questions for your expert

  • What is one healthy habit teenagers should develop?
  • Why do you think young people spend so much time online?
  • What would you recommend to a teenager who cannot stop checking their phone?
  • What will happen if teenagers do not get enough sleep?
  • What would you change if you could redesign social media?
  • What might have happened if people had never invented smartphones?
